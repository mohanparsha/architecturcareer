How this site is maintained

Architect U'r Career is a personal site. I write it to pass on what more than 25 years in enterprise technology have taught me, to students, working engineers and teachers. Free material is only useful if it is right. This page says how I keep it right, and what to do when it is not.

Four commitmentsDisclosureHow pages are reviewedLicenceReport an errorWhat changed

Four commitments

  1. Every statistic carries its source and its year. A number I cannot trace to a source is removed, however useful it sounds.
  2. Every page shows when it was last reviewed. The date is in the strip at the top of each guide. A page that has not been reviewed for more than six months says so in a banner, automatically, so that an old page can never pass as a current one.
  3. Corrections are public. Each guide has a record of what changed, below. If you find an error, tell me: the correction is made in the open and recorded here.
  4. Interests are disclosed. Where I work, and any product I name that I have worked on or with, is stated on this page and beside the mention.

Disclosure

  • I am CTO and Co-Founder of Rhinexa, which builds platforms for governed AI adoption, network monitoring and network detection. This site does not describe or promote those products. Where a guide covers ground close to my work, it stays vendor-neutral. The views here are my own and not those of my employer.
  • Until August 2026 I worked at Orion Governance, the maker of EIIG, which is named once in the Data Intelligence Layer guide.
  • From May 2023 to October 2025 I worked at Payswiff, where my team ran the open-appsec web application firewall. My account of that is published on Check Point's open-appsec blog and linked from the home page.
  • I am a member of the Department Advisory Committee of G. Narayanamma Institute of Technology and Science for Women and of the Board of Governors of SGM Government Polytechnic, both in Hyderabad.
  • The site carries no advertising, sponsorship or affiliate links, and asks for no sign-up. Page views are counted with GoatCounter, which sets no cookies. Some pages load fonts from Google Fonts.
  • I use AI tools to help draft, check and maintain these pages. I review what is published, and I am responsible for every word.

How pages are reviewed

In November 2026 every guide on the site was reviewed line by line against primary sources. The review found well over a hundred errors, out-of-date facts and claims without a source. They were corrected, and the list for each guide is below. One guide was retired and three thin pages were merged into stronger ones, so the site now has eleven guides where it had fifteen.

From here on, the three pages whose facts age fastest (the Cybersecurity Career Roadmap, the AI Knowledge Map and Securing AI Systems and Agents) are reviewed every quarter. Every other guide is reviewed at least twice a year. Prices, exam codes, product names and free tiers change without notice: where a page gives one, it gives the official link beside it, and the official page is the one to trust.

Report an error

If something is wrong, out of date or unclear, open an issue on GitHub (you need a free GitHub account) or send me an email. Say which page, what is wrong, and where you checked it. A source helps. Open and closed reports are public.

Known gaps

Things I know are not yet right or not yet checked. They are listed here so that you know too.

  • Cybersecurity Career Roadmap: the India starting-salary ranges are my own indicative figures. I could not find a published survey to source them to, and the page says so.
  • Network Concepts Guide: two of its four diagrams (encapsulation, and defence in depth) are older drawings awaiting a redraw. The defence-in-depth image contains one unsourced line, which its caption flags.
  • Secure SDLC: the PCI DSS and SOC 2 references in the compliance map were checked against published reproductions, not the licensed originals, and the ISO/IEC 27001 control titles against the contents list of ISO/IEC 27002:2022. The KPI targets and the RACI split are examples, not benchmarks.
  • Operating Systems Unit 1: the textbook's Chapter 2 facts were checked against a university's copy of the authors' slides, one step from the original. The learning-outcome numbers are illustrative and not taken from one university's syllabus.
  • IKS Life Skills Curriculum: two folk stories (Birbal's khichdi, Tenali Rama and the thieves) and one Kabir couplet are marked on the page as traditional, with no verified source. The scenario cards cover every Tier 1 session, and Tier 2 only for Weeks 1, 6, 9 and 10.
  • Telugu and Hindi summaries on the roadmap and the curriculum are short and new. If a phrase reads wrongly, tell me.
  • Testing: every page was checked in one desktop browser at desktop and phone width, and by keyboard. None has yet been tested with a screen reader, in Safari or Firefox, or on a range of real phones.
  • Prices and versions were checked in November 2026 and will drift. Each one sits beside a link to the official page.

Added "Safeguarding notes for facilitators" near the top, and a short safeguarding note inside every week.
  • Removed or reworded other activities that could single out or expose a student: no naming classmates as rivals, no recording of students, private journals stay private, mock trials and disputes use invented cases, and feedback is about the role and never about a student's body, voice, family or background.
  • Corrected the Week 2 story. The Sivi Jataka (no. 499) is about a king who gives his eyes; the king who offers his flesh to a hawk to save a pigeon is a different story, from the Mahabharata, and is now shown separately.
  • Corrected the Week 3 source: "The Monkey and the Wedge" is in Book 2 of the Hitopadesha (and Book 1 of the Panchatantra), not Book 1 of the Hitopadesha.
  • Corrected the Week 9 story of the dice game: Vidura and Vikarna do protest in the assembly, and Draupadi's question is now at the centre of the session. Removed the "loaded dice" detail, which is not in the text.
  • Corrected the Week 8 source (Valmiki's sixth book is the Yuddha Kanda, not "Lanka Kanda") and the Week 7 summary (Krishna does tell Arjuna to fight, and leaves the final choice to him at 18.63).
  • Added a precise citation to every story and verse (text, book or section, verse, and the translation used), a collapsed list of all sources, and the words "Traditional; source not verified" where a source could not be checked.
  • Added companion sources from more traditions and with women at the centre: Gargi and Maitreyi (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad), Savitri (Mahabharata), Thirukkural couplets, a Jain text on non-violence, Amrita Devi of the Bishnoi tradition, Rani Abbakka, Ahilyabai Holkar, and articles of the Constitution of India.
  • Removed 17 dead links (seven of them videos) and corrected or replaced 8 more whose labels did not match where they led (two NPTEL courses, a Gita link described as a translation it was not, two links to the 2019 UGC document labelled as 2.0). They are replaced with links to the texts themselves, all checked in November 2026.
  • Added "How this relates to UGC Jeevan Kaushal 2.0": this is an independent work for Classes 9 to 12, not published or endorsed by UGC, NCERT, CBSE or any government body. The separate "Jeevan Kaushal 2.0" page has been withdrawn and merged here. Removed wording that suggested official alignment.
  • Added a note on fitting the 13 periods into a school timetable, a short summary in Telugu and Hindi, and links to the printable scenario cards.
  • Added the shared site header and footer, a licence (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), keyboard access to the phase filters, and language tags for Sanskrit, Tamil, Hindi and Telugu text. Removed every em dash.

IKS Life Skills: scenario cards and facilitator notes version 1.0, November 2026

  • Added a printable pack of 67 original scenario cards for the 12-week IKS Life Skills curriculum (matched to curriculum version 1.1), so a teacher no longer has to write cards before a session.
  • Added a one-page facilitator note for every week: the aim, minute-by-minute timings for one period, follow-up questions, what a thoughtful response tends to do, and how many copies to print.
  • Added Tier 2 card sets where the curriculum names cards: private briefs for the playing-field negotiation (Week 1), listener cards (Week 6) and event cards (Week 10).
  • Added the written briefs for the Week 9 bystander role-play, which is openly announced, uses volunteers with invented names, runs twice and has an opt-out.
  • Added an opening page on printing and cutting, grouping students, ground rules for discussion and safeguarding, with the Child Helpline number 1098 checked against the Ministry of Women and Child Development page on 18 November 2026.
  • Prints on 34 A4 pages in black and white, four cards to a page with cut lines; each week can also be printed on its own.

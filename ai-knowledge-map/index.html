  1. 1Step 1: 🧠 Artificial intelligence (AI)

    The widest box: getting machines to do things that seem to need intelligence

    Artificial intelligence is the field of building computer systems that carry out tasks we normally associate with human intelligence: learning from experience, understanding language, recognising patterns, planning and making decisions. It is a field of study with many branches, not one technology. It runs from the hand-written rules of 1980s expert systems to today's machine learning and agents.

    Key points

    1. The name comes from the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, a workshop organised by John McCarthy in 1956.
    2. Its branches include machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, robotics, planning and search, and rule-based (knowledge) systems.
    3. Narrow AI does one kind of task, such as filtering spam or recognising speech. General AI would match people across most tasks. Nearly everything in use today is narrow. Whether the newest general-purpose models count as general intelligence is argued over, and there is no agreed test.
    4. Not all AI learns. A route planner or a chess program can be built from search and rules alone. The kind that learns is machine learning, the next step.

    Examples

    • Route planning in a maps app Search algorithms find a path, and predictions from past and live traffic estimate the journey time.
    • Email spam filters Sort incoming mail into wanted and unwanted by its patterns. An early everyday use of machine learning.
    • Driver assistance in cars Combines computer vision with planning to hold a lane and keep a safe distance. The driver remains responsible.

    How it connects

    • Machine learning is a branch of AI.
    • NLP is a branch of AI.

    Everything else on this map sits inside AI or is built from parts of it. Read the next steps as a zoom inwards: machine learning, then deep learning, then generative AI, then large language models.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Dartmouth College on the 1956 workshop.

  2. 2Step 2: 📊 Machine learning (ML)

    Learning the rules from data, where a programmer would otherwise write them

    Machine learning is the branch of AI in which a system gets better at a task by finding patterns in data. You do not write the rule 'if the temperature is above 30 degrees, switch on the cooling'. You give the system many past examples and it works out the thresholds itself. Most of the AI in the news today is machine learning.

    Key points

    1. Supervised learning: you supply examples with the right answer attached (photos labelled 'cat' or 'dog') and the model learns to predict the answer for new cases.
    2. Unsupervised learning: no answers are supplied. The model finds structure by itself, such as groups of similar customers in purchase records.
    3. Reinforcement learning: the system acts, receives a reward or a penalty, and adjusts. A program can learn a board game this way by playing against itself.
    4. Self-supervised learning: the labels come from the data itself, for example by hiding the next word of a sentence and asking the model to predict it. This is how large language models are pre-trained.
    5. Common model families: linear and logistic regression, decision trees and random forests, gradient boosting, support vector machines, k-means clustering and neural networks.
    6. A model is only as good as its data. Gaps and bias in the examples become gaps and bias in the predictions.

    Examples

    • Recommendations Streaming and shopping services suggest what to watch or buy next, from your history and from people with similar tastes.
    • Card fraud detection Models trained on past transactions flag a payment that does not fit the customer's usual pattern, while the payment is being made.
    • Predictive maintenance Sensor readings from machines are used to predict a failure before it happens, so that the repair can be planned.

    How it connects

    • Machine learning is a branch of AI.
    • Deep learning is a kind of machine learning.

    Deep learning, the next step, is the part of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers.

  3. 3Step 3: 🔗 Neural networks and deep learning

    Layers of simple units that learn their own features

    A neural network is a model made of layers of simple connected units, loosely inspired by the neurons of the brain. Each connection has a weight, a number that training adjusts. Deep learning means neural networks with many layers. The early layers pick up simple features, such as edges in a photograph, and the later layers combine them into complex ones, such as a face.

    Key points

    1. Shape: an input layer, one or more hidden layers and an output layer. Each layer transforms what the previous one produced.
    2. Training: the network makes a prediction and the error is measured. Backpropagation works out how much each weight contributed to the error, and gradient descent nudges every weight to reduce it. This is repeated over a very large number of examples.
    3. Main architectures: convolutional networks (CNNs) for images, recurrent networks (RNNs and LSTMs) for sequences, and Transformers, which now lead in language and are widely used for images and audio too.
    4. The Transformer was introduced in the 2017 paper 'Attention Is All You Need' by Ashish Vaswani and colleagues. Its self-attention mechanism lets every part of the input be weighed against every other part.
    5. Deep learning needs a great deal of data and computing power, usually graphics processors (GPUs) or other specialised chips.

    Examples

    • Medical imaging Convolutional networks highlight possible tumours in X-rays and scans for a radiologist to review.
    • Speech recognition Voice assistants and dictation tools use neural networks to turn speech into text.
    • AlphaFold DeepMind's network predicts the three-dimensional shape of proteins. In July 2022 its public database grew to over 200 million predicted structures.

    How it connects

    • Deep learning is a kind of machine learning.
    • Generative AI is built on deep learning (neural networks).

    The Transformer is the architecture behind the large language models in step 6.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Vaswani and others, 'Attention Is All You Need' (2017); Google DeepMind on the AlphaFold database (July 2022).

  4. 4Step 4: 💬 Natural language processing (NLP)

    Getting computers to work with human language

    Natural language processing is the branch of AI concerned with human language, written and spoken: understanding it, producing it and translating it. Language is hard for machines because it is ambiguous and depends on context. People use sarcasm and idiom, and often mean more than they say. NLP is defined by the problem and not by a technique, which is why the map places it inside AI but outside machine learning: the earliest systems were hand-written rules.

    Key points

    1. Typical tasks: splitting text into tokens, tagging parts of speech, finding the names of people and places (named entity recognition), judging sentiment, translating, summarising and answering questions.
    2. Four stages: hand-written rules (1950s to 1980s), statistical methods (1990s and 2000s), neural networks (2010s) and Transformers (2017 onwards).
    3. Word embeddings such as Word2Vec and GloVe represent each word as a list of numbers, placed so that words with similar meanings sit close together.
    4. Transformers read all the words of a passage together, using self-attention. Earlier networks read one word at a time.
    5. Large language models now handle most of these tasks with one general model, where each task once needed a model of its own.

    Examples

    • Machine translation In June 2024 Google Translate added 110 languages in a single release, using a large language model.
    • Sentiment analysis Companies sort large volumes of reviews and social media posts into positive, negative and neutral.
    • Contract review Tools pull clauses, obligations and dates out of long legal documents for a lawyer to check.

    How it connects

    • NLP is a branch of AI.
    • An LLM is used for NLP.

    Almost all NLP today is done with machine learning, and most of it with the large language models of step 6.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Google, '110 new languages are coming to Google Translate' (June 2024).

  5. 5Step 5: ✨ Generative AI

    Models that produce new content: text, images, audio, video and code

    Generative AI is the name for models that produce new content, where earlier systems mostly sorted or scored existing data. A generative model learns the patterns in a very large collection of text, images, audio or video. It then produces new output that follows those patterns without copying any single example. Today's generative models are deep neural networks, which is why this box sits inside deep learning on the map.

    Key points

    1. Main families: large language models for text and code; diffusion models, the technique behind many image and video generators (Stable Diffusion is a published example); and the earlier GANs and VAEs.
    2. A foundation model is a large model trained once on broad data and then adapted to many tasks.
    3. Many current models are multimodal: one model accepts or produces more than one kind of content, such as text and images.
    4. Generative AI became widely known when ChatGPT was released in November 2022. A UBS study reported by Reuters estimated that it reached 100 million monthly users two months later.
    5. A generative model produces what is likely, not what is true. Its output needs checking, and it raises questions of copyright, consent and deepfakes.

    Examples

    • Chat assistants ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini write, summarise, explain and translate text in conversation.
    • Image generation A description such as 'a lighthouse at dawn, in watercolour' is turned into a picture.
    • Coding assistants Tools such as GitHub Copilot suggest code, explain it and make changes across a project.

    How it connects

    • Generative AI is built on deep learning (neural networks).
    • An LLM is one kind of generative AI.

    Agentic AI (step 11) takes generative models one stage further: from producing content when asked, to carrying out tasks.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Rombach and others, the latent diffusion paper behind Stable Diffusion (2021); Reuters report of the UBS estimate, carried by Euronews (2 February 2023).

  6. 6Step 6: 📝 Large language models (LLMs)

    Trained to predict the next token, and far more capable than that sounds

    A large language model is a very large Transformer network trained on a very large amount of text. Its basic job is simple: given the text so far, predict the next token (a word or part of a word). Repeating that step produces whole answers. Done at sufficient scale, this one skill yields abilities that nobody programmed in directly: answering questions, writing code, translating, summarising and working through a problem step by step.

    Key points

    1. Tokens: text is cut into tokens before the model sees it. Length limits and prices are counted in tokens, not words.
    2. Size is counted in parameters, the learned weights. 'Large' has no fixed threshold, and many vendors do not publish the figure for their models.
    3. Training order: first, pre-training on raw text by next-token prediction. Second, supervised fine-tuning on example instructions with good answers. Third, further tuning from human feedback on the model's own answers (RLHF and related methods). The InstructGPT paper describes the second and third stages.
    4. Context window: the amount of text the model can take in at one time, counted in tokens. Early generative models handled about 8,000 tokens. Several current models accept a million or more. Check the documentation of the model you use.
    5. Limits: a model can state something false with complete fluency (hallucination). It knows nothing after its training cut-off date. It cannot look anything up or take any action unless it is connected to tools. Steps 7 to 10 deal with those limits.
    6. Models are released in families and replaced often. Learn the ideas, and look up the current version when you need it.

    Examples

    • Hosted model families GPT (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic) and Gemini (Google) are offered through chat applications and through programming interfaces (APIs). Each family comes in several sizes that trade capability against speed and cost.
    • Open-weight models Some developers publish the trained weights, so that a model can be downloaded and run on your own hardware. Hubs such as Hugging Face list them.
    • Small language models Cut-down models that run on a laptop or a phone, trading some ability for privacy, speed and cost.

    How it connects

    • An LLM is one kind of generative AI.
    • An LLM is used for NLP.
    • RAG supplies facts to an LLM.
    • An AI agent is built on an LLM.

    Technically an LLM is a neural network (step 3) of the Transformer type. It is the reasoning engine inside every later item on this map.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Ouyang and others, the InstructGPT paper (2022); Google, long context in the Gemini API; Anthropic, Claude models overview; OpenAI, models.

  7. 7Step 7: 🔍 Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

    Look it up first, then answer

    Retrieval-augmented generation is a design pattern, not a model. Before the LLM answers, the system searches a store of documents for passages relevant to the question and places them in the prompt, so that the model answers from that material. The name comes from a 2020 paper by Patrick Lewis and colleagues. RAG reduces three weaknesses of an LLM used alone: invented facts, out-of-date knowledge and ignorance of your organisation's own documents. It reduces them. It does not remove them.

    Key points

    1. Three steps: retrieve (search the document store), augment (add what was found to the prompt), generate (the LLM writes an answer from it).
    2. Embeddings: an embedding model turns each passage into a list of numbers, so that passages with similar meaning sit close together. This allows search by meaning, not only by matching words.
    3. The passages are kept in a vector index, either in a dedicated vector database or as a feature of an ordinary database. Good systems combine meaning-based search with keyword search and then re-rank the results.
    4. Preparation matters as much as the model. How the documents are split into chunks, cleaned and labelled decides what can be found.
    5. The knowledge can be updated without retraining the model: change the documents and the next answer uses them.
    6. Retrieval must respect permissions. A user should only receive answers drawn from documents that user is allowed to read.

    Examples

    • Staff policy assistant Answers HR questions from the organisation's own policy documents and shows which document each answer came from.
    • Legal research Finds the relevant cases and statutes first, then drafts an analysis that cites them, for a lawyer to verify.
    • Product support Pulls the current documentation and known issues, so that troubleshooting advice matches the latest release.

    How it connects

    • RAG supplies facts to an LLM.
    • An AI agent uses RAG.

    Retrieval relies on embedding models, which are neural networks (step 3). Two variants you will meet: GraphRAG retrieves from a knowledge graph, and in agentic RAG an agent decides what to search for and when. A document store can also be offered to an assistant as an MCP server (step 9). For the data foundations, see the Data Intelligence Layer guide.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Lewis and others, 'Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks' (2020).

  8. 8Step 8: 🤖 AI agent

    An LLM that can act, not only answer

    An AI agent is a software system built around an LLM that works towards a goal by taking actions. A chatbot answers one prompt at a time. An agent breaks a task into steps, chooses a tool for each step, looks at the result and decides what to do next, until the goal is met or it reaches a limit. The LLM does the reasoning. The program around it, often called the runtime or harness, runs the tools and enforces the limits.

    Key points

    1. The loop: perceive (take in the goal and the context), plan, act (call a tool), observe the result and adapt. Repeat until the goal is met or the budget of steps, time or money runs out.
    2. Tools turn text into action: calling an API, querying a database, running code, searching the web, reading and writing files.
    3. Memory: working memory is whatever is in the context window now. Longer-term stores keep facts, past episodes and learned procedures between sessions.
    4. Limits are part of the design: which tools the agent may use, what it must ask a person to approve, and a record of everything it did.
    5. Frameworks change quickly. Three in use in November 2026: LangGraph, Google's Agent Development Kit and Microsoft Agent Framework, the successor to AutoGen, which is now in maintenance mode.
    6. An agent can be attacked through what it reads: a web page or a document can carry instructions aimed at the agent (prompt injection). See the Securing AI Systems and Agents guide.

    Examples

    • Coding agent Given a task, it reads the repository, edits several files, runs the tests, fixes what failed and proposes the change for review.
    • Customer support agent Looks up the order, applies the refund policy, takes the permitted action and hands over to a person when the case is outside its limits.
    • Research agent Searches for sources, reads them, compares what they say and writes a report with citations.

    How it connects

    • An AI agent is built on an LLM.
    • An AI agent uses RAG.
    • An AI agent reaches tools and data through MCP.
    • An AI agent talks to other agents through A2A.
    • Agentic AI is built from AI agents.

    For how an agent runtime is put together, see the Agentic AI Runtime Architecture guide.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: AutoGen repository notice on maintenance mode.

  9. 9Step 9: 🔌 Model Context Protocol (MCP)

    One standard way to plug tools and data into an AI application

    The Model Context Protocol is an open protocol for connecting LLM applications to external data sources and tools. Anthropic published it in November 2024. Before it, every pairing of an AI application and a tool needed its own connector. With a shared protocol, a tool is wrapped once as an MCP server and any compatible application can use it. The project's own documentation compares it to a USB-C port for AI applications. Since December 2025 MCP has been an open project of the Agentic AI Foundation, part of the Linux Foundation.

    Key points (as of november 2026)

    1. Three roles. The host is the AI application the user sees. The host runs one client for each connection, and each client talks to exactly one server. A server exposes one focused set of data or tools.
    2. A server can offer three things: resources (data to read), prompts (reusable templates) and tools (functions the model can call).
    3. Wire format: messages are JSON-RPC 2.0. There are two standard transports: stdio for a server running on the same machine, and Streamable HTTP for a remote one. Authorisation for HTTP servers is optional and based on OAuth 2.1.
    4. Current revision: 2026-07-28. It made the protocol stateless. The opening 'initialize' handshake and protocol-level sessions were removed, and every request now carries its own protocol version and capabilities. Long-running Tasks moved into an official extension, and the Roots, Sampling and Logging features are deprecated. Anything written before mid-2026 describes the older, session-based design.
    5. Adoption: in December 2025 Anthropic reported more than 10,000 active public MCP servers, and support in ChatGPT, Cursor, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Visual Studio Code.
    6. Security: a tool is code that runs on the model's say-so. The specification tells hosts to obtain the user's consent before a tool is invoked, and to treat a tool's description of itself as untrusted unless it comes from a trusted server.

    Examples

    • A chat assistant with connectors The assistant reads a code repository, searches a team chat and queries a shared drive, each through its own MCP server.
    • A coding assistant in an editor MCP servers give the assistant access to project files, documentation and a database, under the user's control.
    • Enterprise data A company wraps its customer system or data warehouse as an MCP server once, and every approved AI application can use it.

    How it connects

    • MCP works alongside A2A.
    • An AI agent reaches tools and data through MCP.

    MCP connects one agent to its tools and data. A2A (step 10) connects one agent to another agent. They are designed to be used together. Read the MCP specification, revision 2026-07-28.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: MCP versioning page (current revision); MCP changelog for 2026-07-28; MCP documentation, introduction; Anthropic, 'Introducing the Model Context Protocol' (25 November 2024); Anthropic on donating MCP to the Agentic AI Foundation (9 December 2025).

  10. 10Step 10: 🤝 Agent2Agent Protocol (A2A)

    A standard way for one agent to hand work to another

    The Agent2Agent (A2A) Protocol is an open protocol that lets AI agents built by different teams, on different frameworks, find each other and work on tasks together. Google introduced it in April 2025 and the Linux Foundation became its home in June 2025. Version 1.0, the first stable specification, was released in March 2026. In August 2026 the project joined the Agentic AI Foundation, alongside MCP.

    Key points (as of november 2026)

    1. Two roles. A client agent asks for work and a remote agent (the A2A server) does it. The client sees the remote agent only from the outside: how it works, what it remembers and which tools it uses are not exposed.
    2. Agent Card: a JSON document in which an agent states its identity, skills, endpoint and authentication requirements. By convention it is published at /.well-known/agent-card.json. Before version 0.3 the file was called agent.json, which older tutorials still show.
    3. Task: the unit of work, with a defined life cycle. A task is submitted, then working, and it ends as completed, failed, cancelled or rejected.
    4. Two states pause a task: input-required (the remote agent needs more information) and auth-required (it needs further authorisation). Being able to stop and ask is what separates delegating to an agent from calling a function.
    5. Three ways to get the result: ask and poll, receive a stream of updates, or register a webhook and be notified, which suits long-running tasks.
    6. Three protocol bindings that must behave the same: JSON-RPC, gRPC and HTTP with JSON.

    Examples

    • Hiring A hiring agent delegates to specialists: one sources candidates, another schedules interviews, a third requests background checks.
    • Supply chain A stock agent notices a shortage and asks suppliers' agents for quotes. A person approves the order.
    • Across vendors' platforms Agents inside a customer system, a service desk and a finance system each handle their own part of one customer escalation.

    How it connects

    • An AI agent talks to other agents through A2A.
    • MCP works alongside A2A.

    The A2A project describes the split this way: MCP connects each agent to its own tools, and A2A connects the agents to each other. Read the A2A specification, version 1.0.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: A2A releases (v1.0.0, 12 March 2026); A2A key concepts; A2A and MCP; Linux Foundation announcement (23 June 2025); Agentic AI Foundation, 'A2A joins AAIF' (17 August 2026).

  11. 11Step 11: ⚡ Agentic AI

    From AI that answers to AI that carries out work

    Agentic AI is an approach, not a product: building systems in which AI agents pursue goals with a degree of independence, inside limits that people set. 'AI agent' names one worker. 'Agentic AI' names the whole arrangement: several agents, the orchestration that coordinates them, the protocols that connect them and the governance that keeps them within bounds.

    Key points

    1. What makes a system agentic: it works towards a goal, plans its own steps, uses tools to act, adapts when a step fails, and needs less step-by-step instruction than a chatbot.
    2. Difference from generative AI: a generative model produces content when asked. An agentic system decides what to do next and does it.
    3. Common shapes: one orchestrating agent directing specialists; peer agents passing work between themselves; agent features built into existing software.
    4. Autonomy is a dial, not a switch. Sensible deployments keep a person approving the steps that matter.
    5. Shared standards have a common home. The Agentic AI Foundation, part of the Linux Foundation, was founded in December 2025 by Anthropic, Block and OpenAI. In November 2026 its projects include MCP, A2A and AGENTS.md.
    6. Adoption: in a survey of 2,102 people run in spring 2025 by MIT Sloan Management Review and BCG, 35% said their organisation had adopted agentic AI and a further 44% planned to deploy it.
    7. Action brings new risk. An agent that can be tricked can do damage, not only say something wrong, so identity, permissions, approval and audit matter as much as the quality of the model.

    Examples

    • IT operations Agents watch the systems, diagnose a fault, propose or apply a fix within agreed limits, confirm that it worked and write the incident report.
    • Procurement Agents spot a need from stock levels, compare suppliers, request quotes from suppliers' agents over A2A and prepare the purchase order for approval.
    • Clinical decision support Agents gather a patient's records and current research and draft options for the clinician, who decides.

    How it connects

    • Agentic AI is built from AI agents.

    Agentic AI is where the whole map comes together: LLMs reason, RAG supplies knowledge, MCP connects tools, A2A connects agents, and agents do the work. It extends generative AI (step 5) from producing content to taking action.

    Sources, checked in November 2026: Anthropic on the founding of the Agentic AI Foundation (9 December 2025); Agentic AI Foundation, projects; MIT Sloan Management Review and BCG, 'The Emerging Agentic Enterprise' (18 November 2025).

Check yourself

Answer each question in your head, then open it.

Is every AI system a machine learning system?

No. Machine learning is one branch of AI. A route planner built from search and rules is AI without any learning.

Starting from an LLM, which boxes do you pass through on the way out to AI?

Generative AI, then deep learning (neural networks), then machine learning, then AI.

Does RAG change the model or the prompt?

The prompt. The model is untouched. The passages that were retrieved are added to the prompt, and the model answers from them.

An agent needs to query a database, and to hand a task to another team's agent. Which protocol serves each?

MCP for the database, because it connects an agent to tools and data. A2A for the other agent, because it connects agents to each other.

What is the difference between an AI agent and agentic AI?

An AI agent is one worker: an LLM with tools, working towards a goal. Agentic AI is the whole approach: several agents, their orchestration, the protocols between them and the limits that people set.

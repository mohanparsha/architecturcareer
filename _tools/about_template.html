How this site is maintained

Architect U'r Career is a personal site. I write it to pass on what more than 25 years in enterprise technology have taught me, to students, working engineers and teachers. Free material is only useful if it is right. This page says how I keep it right, and what to do when it is not.

Four commitments

  1. Every statistic carries its source and its year. A number I cannot trace to a source is removed, however useful it sounds.
  2. Every page shows when it was last reviewed. The date is in the strip at the top of each guide. A page that has not been reviewed for more than six months says so in a banner, automatically, so that an old page can never pass as a current one.
  3. Corrections are public. Each guide has a record of what changed, below. If you find an error, tell me: the correction is made in the open and recorded here.
  4. Interests are disclosed. Where I work, and any product I name that I have worked on or with, is stated on this page and beside the mention.

How pages are reviewed

In November 2026 every guide on the site was reviewed line by line against primary sources. The review found well over a hundred errors, out-of-date facts and claims without a source. They were corrected, and the list for each guide is below. One guide was retired and three thin pages were merged into stronger ones, so the site now has eleven guides where it had fifteen.

From here on, the three pages whose facts age fastest (the Cybersecurity Career Roadmap, the AI Knowledge Map and Securing AI Systems and Agents) are reviewed every quarter. Every other guide is reviewed at least twice a year. Prices, exam codes, product names and free tiers change without notice: where a page gives one, it gives the official link beside it, and the official page is the one to trust.

Report an error

If something is wrong, out of date or unclear, open an issue on GitHub (you need a free GitHub account) or send me an email. Say which page, what is wrong, and where you checked it. A source helps. Open and closed reports are public.

What changed

The site, November 2026

  • The home page now presents the guides as three learning paths: start a security career, secure and govern AI, and teach it.
  • Every guide moved to a stable address with no version number in it. The old addresses redirect.
  • Every guide gained the same header strip (home link, review date) and footer (licence, error report, this page), and its controls now work from the keyboard.
  • The material is now licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. It was "all rights reserved", which meant a teacher could not legally photocopy it.
  • Retired: the LLM Deep Dive. It contained errors I chose to remove and not patch, and others teach the subject better. Its old address says so and points to them.
  • Merged: the Network Glossary into the Network Concepts Guide, the ten soft skills into Thinking Skills, and the Jeevan Kaushal page into the IKS Life Skills Curriculum, where it now states plainly that the curriculum is independent of UGC.
  • Rewritten: the AI Security Stack, as Securing AI Systems and Agents.
  • Two network infographics were wrong (one mislabelled the attack chain, the other put address resolution after the TLS handshake). Both were redrawn.
  • Removed from the home page: the reader quotes. They were genuine, and the readers asked not to be named. This site now publishes only what a reader can check.

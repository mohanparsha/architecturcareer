  • Post-quantum migration: NIST deprecates RSA/ECC after 2030, disallows 2035

Application Security

D2
Protects → Software & Applications
OWASP Top 10:2025Burp SuiteSAST/DASTSupply ChainAPI SecurityASPM
Certification Path
BSCP→HTB CWES→OSWE→CSSLP

Key Skills

  • OWASP Top 10:2025, covering Broken Access Control (#1, the home of IDOR, insecure direct object references, and now SSRF), Software Supply Chain Failures (new A03), and Injection (SQL injection and XSS, cross-site scripting)
  • Secure coding: input validation, parameterised queries
  • SAST / DAST / SCA: Burp Suite, ZAP (now ZAP by Checkmarx, no longer an OWASP project), SonarQube, Semgrep
  • API security: OAuth 2.0, JWT vulnerabilities, REST/GraphQL (OWASP API Top 10)
  • DevSecOps: CI/CD pipeline security, SBOM generation, dependency pinning (this site's Secure SDLC guide and DevOps and DevSecOps Roadmap cover both)
  • Reviewing and securing AI-generated code, a fast-growing AppSec workload

2026 Watch

  • Cert path changed: CEH remains an HR/compliance filter, but practical credentials (BSCP, HTB CWES, OSWE) are what AppSec hiring managers now look for. The BSCP exam is $99, but you also need an active Burp Suite Professional subscription ($499) to sit it (PortSwigger, checked November 2026)
  • ASPM (Application Security Posture Management) is a term to know: one prioritised view across SAST, DAST, SCA and runtime findings

  • Securing AI agent identities is the newest frontier; it overlaps with Domain 8

SOC & Incident Response

D6
Protects → All Assets (24/7 Detection)
SIEMMITRE ATT&CK v20Detection-as-CodeDFIRSigmaAI Triage
Certification Path
SAL1 / CySA+→BTL1→SC-200→GCIH

Key Skills

  • SIEM: Wazuh, Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel, Elastic
  • Detection engineering: Sigma rules, YARA, detection-as-code in CI/CD (a distinct, well-paid role)
  • IR lifecycle: learn the plain five steps first, because interviewers still ask for them: Prepare, Detect, Contain, Eradicate, Recover. Then read NIST SP 800-61r3 (2025), which reorganises the same work around CSF 2.0's six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
  • DFIR basics: Volatility (memory), Autopsy (disk)
  • MITRE ATT&CK: TTPs, IOC analysis, Detection Strategies & Analytics (the v18 model that replaced legacy Detections)
  • AI oversight: verifying and correcting agentic AI triage output

2026 Watch

  • Agentic AI is reshaping Tier-1. Repetitive alert triage is shrinking; a first-year analyst in 2026 does work that resembled year-two work in 2023. Build automation, scripting and investigation-verification skills, not alert-queue endurance
  • ATT&CK v18 (Oct 2025) replaced Detections with Detection Strategies; v19 (Apr 2026) split Defense Evasion into two tactics; v20 (13 Oct 2026) is current and adds techniques for direct and indirect prompt injection, which ties this domain to Domain 8 (MITRE)
  • TryHackMe SAL1 is gaining genuine traction with SOC hiring managers as a budget entry credential

Key Skills

  • Policy writing: InfoSec policies, standards, procedures
  • Audit & TPRM: internal audits, third-party risk, vendor assessments
  • AI governance: ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act readiness (a fast-growing GRC lane)

India Regulatory Stack (2026)

  • DPDP Rules, 2025: notified Nov 2025. Phased: the Data Protection Board provisions applied from Nov 2025; the consent-manager provisions were scheduled to apply from 13 Nov 2026 (confirm the current position on MeitY's site); most substantive obligations apply from 13 May 2027. Knowing the Act alone is no longer enough
  • SEBI CSCRF: in force since Aug 2025 for most regulated entities; mandates SOC coverage, VAPT and annual cyber audits. A genuine India hiring driver
  • CERT-In: 2022 Directions still binding (6-hour incident reporting, 180-day log retention in India) + 2025 Cyber Security Audit Policy Guidelines covering AI systems, IoT and SBOM/AIBOM
  • RBI Master Direction on IT Governance (effective Apr 2024) anchors BFSI; Telecom Cyber Security Rules (2024) for telcos

Global Track

  • ISO 27001:2022: the :2013 transition closed 31 Oct 2025; old certificates are now invalid
  • PCI DSS v4.0.1: all future-dated requirements mandatory since Mar 2025
  • EU AI Act: prohibitions & GPAI obligations live; high-risk deadlines moved to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028
  • DORA (applicable Jan 2025) and NIS2 for EU; SEC cyber-disclosure rules and CIRCIA for US
  • The CISM exam has followed a new content outline since 3 Nov 2026 (ISACA), so check that study material matches it; the CISSP experience-waiver list narrowed in Apr 2026

AI Security & Governance

D8
Protects → AI Models, LLM Apps & Agents
LLM Top 10Prompt InjectionMITRE ATLASAI Red TeamingISO 42001Agentic AI
Certification Path
Security+→SecAI+ / GOAA→AIGP→AAISM

Why This Is Now Domain 8

  • AI is the most-cited skills need in the ISC2 2025 Cybersecurity Workforce Study (41% of respondents), and 73% said AI will create more specialised cybersecurity skills
  • Only 14% of organisations are confident they have the people and skills they need (World Economic Forum, Global Cybersecurity Outlook 2025)
  • MITRE ATT&CK v20 (Oct 2026) added prompt-injection techniques, so AI attacks are now part of mainstream detection work

Key Skills

  • OWASP GenAI/LLM Top 10 (2026 edition): prompt injection is still #1; Excessive Agency rising
  • OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (2026): goal hijacking, tool misuse, memory/context poisoning
  • MITRE ATLAS: the ATT&CK equivalent for adversarial ML, now including agentic-AI techniques
  • AI red teaming: jailbreaks, data/model poisoning, model extraction, guardrail bypass
  • Tooling: Microsoft PyRIT, NVIDIA garak, guardrail frameworks
  • Securing the AI supply chain: model provenance, AIBOM, third-party model risk
  • AI governance: ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF + GenAI Profile, EU AI Act obligations

Certifications That Actually Exist (2026)

  • CompTIA SecAI+ (exam CY0-001): launched Feb 2026, the accessible technical entry point. Voucher $209 on CompTIA's store with India selected (checked November 2026)
  • GIAC GOAA (Offensive AI Analyst) and GAIPS (AI Platform Security): hands-on, premium priced
  • IAPP AIGP: a widely recognised AI governance credential. Exam $799, or $649 for IAPP members (IAPP, checked November 2026)
  • ISACA AAISM (AI Security Management): requires an active CISM or CISSP, so it is a senior rung, not an entry point
  • Microsoft SC-500: cloud + AI security engineering (the successor to the retired AZ-500)
  • Portfolio note: for AI red team roles, CTF wins and open-source contributions currently outweigh certificates

Free Ways to Start Tonight

  • Lakera Gandalf: Agent Breaker: a browser game in which you attack GenAI apps with prompt injection. The original Gandalf password game now redirects here
  • PortSwigger Web LLM Attacks labs: free, with live models
  • HTB Academy AI Red Teamer path (built with Google, aligned to SAIF)
  • Run garak or PyRIT against a local open-weights model and write up the findings
  • On this site: Securing AI Systems and Agents and Agentic AI Runtime Architecture

The Convergence Advantage

The highest-value cybersecurity professionals are those who cross domain boundaries: an Offensive Security specialist who understands Cloud, an AppSec engineer who can read GRC risk reports, a SOC analyst who understands IAM. In 2026 there is a second convergence layered on top: every domain now has an AI dimension, and identity has become the centre of gravity for attackers. Plan your foundation first, specialise deeply in one domain, then deliberately acquire adjacent domain knowledge after 3 years. This combination is what the senior roles in the table above ask for, and it leads to the most interesting work.

Two Paths This Table Doesn't Show

Analyst → Engineer. The market increasingly rewards building over monitoring. Security engineering (automation, detection-as-code, tooling) is where analysts usually go to progress. Learn Python and Git from month one and you can make this jump at the 2-3 year mark.

OT / ICS Security. Industrial and critical-infrastructure security is an under-supplied specialism; the SANS 2026 State of ICS/OT Security survey covers its staffing problem. There are very few true entry-level OT roles, so treat this as a deliberate mid-career pivot from network security or SOC after 3-5 years, not as a first job.

18-Month Roadmap

Click any phase to expand its action items. Months 1-6 cost nothing. From month 7 you will need an exam budget, so plan for it early: the rupee budget sits below the timeline.

Months 1-3

Foundation: Build Your Base

Networking, Linux, and scripting fundamentals. Entirely free.

  • Complete the TryHackMe Pre-Security learning path (free)
  • Complete Networking Basics on Cisco Networking Academy (free)
  • Master Linux CLI basics via OverTheWire Bandit
  • Start Python scripting with Automate the Boring Stuff: this is the skill that later separates engineers from analysts
  • Join the null community chapter in your city and set up Git/GitHub
  • Budget note: ISC2's free CC programme ended in May 2026. CC now costs $199 plus a $50 annual maintenance fee (about ₹18,700 plus ₹4,700 a year; ISC2 exam pricing, checked November 2026), so treat it as an optional paid target, not the free win it used to be
Months 4-6

Domain Selection: Choose Your Path

Pick your domain from the self-assessment. Begin domain-specific labs and certification prep.

  • Use the "Find Your Fit" quiz to confirm your domain
  • Start domain-specific labs on TryHackMe / HackTheBox
  • Work through PortSwigger Web Security Academy: free, and the best AppSec training available at any price
  • Spend one weekend on AI security fundamentals: Lakera's Gandalf: Agent Breaker, then read the OWASP LLM Top 10. Every domain now touches this
  • Identify your entry-level certification target and start saving for the exam fee
  • Follow 10+ domain practitioners on LinkedIn & X; watch conference talks on YouTube
Months 7-9

First Certification: Prove Your Skills

Sit CompTIA Security+ or a hands-on domain cert. Attend your first community meetup. Join a CTF team.

  • Sit Security+, or go straight to a practical domain cert (SAL1, eJPT, PJPT, BSCP). SY0-801 is the current Security+ exam; SY0-701 can still be taken in English until 11 Jun 2027. Professor Messer's free course covered SY0-701 when checked in November 2026, so match your study material to the exam code on your voucher
  • Attend your first null community / OWASP chapter meetup
  • Join a CTF team: picoCTF, TryHackMe competitions
  • Start documenting your learning journey publicly from day one of this phase
  • Ask one practitioner 3-5 years ahead for 15 minutes of advice
Months 10-12

Portfolio Building: Show Your Work

In the 2026 market this is the phase that gets you hired. Certificates open the screen; the portfolio wins the interview.

  • Build a home lab and document it: a working SIEM (Wazuh or Elastic) ingesting real logs is the single highest-signal student project
  • Document 5+ lab writeups on GitHub with clear methodology, not just flags
  • Write 2-3 incident-response or investigation walkthroughs: show your reasoning, including what you ruled out
  • Start a technical blog (Medium, Hashnode, or your own site)
  • Contribute to an open-source security tool; even documentation counts
  • Automate something with Python and publish it. Recruiters can tell the difference between a tool user and a tool builder
  • Get your first peer review: ask a community member to read one writeup or project and tell you what is unclear or wrong, then fix it
Months 13-15

Internship & Bug Bounty: Real World Exposure

Apply for security internships, register on bug bounty platforms, attend Nullcon, c0c0n or a BSides event.

  • Apply for security internships (GCCs, Big 4, product companies, consultancies): an internship is the strongest single differentiator in a tight entry market
  • Register on HackerOne / Bugcrowd / Intigriti and start with beginner-friendly programmes
  • Consider AI-focused bug bounty programmes: HackerOne's 2025 report recorded a 540% rise in valid prompt-injection reports
  • Attend Nullcon, c0c0n, Seasides or a BSides; volunteer if tickets are tight
  • Build relationships with 3+ practitioners in your domain
Months 16-18

Job-Ready: Launch Your Career

Second certification, polished portfolio, optimised LinkedIn, active network, and targeted applications.

  • Earn a second, more specialised certification in your domain
  • Have 2-3 strong portfolio projects you can walk through end-to-end in an interview
  • Be able to explain how you would use, and check, an AI security assistant. This now comes up in interviews
  • Optimise LinkedIn: headline, summary, skills, endorsements
  • Apply to target companies: GCCs, Big 4, BFSI, product security teams, MSSPs
  • Referrals beat applications. Every meetup conversation is worth more than ten portal submissions

⏳ Job-Ready Is Not the Same as Job-Landed

Eighteen months of consistent effort will make you genuinely employable; that part of this plan holds up. What has changed is the market on the other side of it. In the ISC2 2025 Cybersecurity Workforce Study, 39% of respondents reported hiring freezes and 36% reported budget cuts in their security teams, and many postings labelled entry-level ask for a few years of experience. A new hire takes months to become independent, which is exactly why employers screen so hard on evidence.

My advice: plan for the search itself to add 3-6 months on top of month 18, and treat that as normal rather than as failure. The candidates who compress it are the ones with an internship, a documented portfolio, and people in the industry who know their name.

Entry-Level Reality Check

An honest picture beats a motivational one. None of this means don't start; it means start differently.

📉 The Market Tightened in 2025-26

  • The ISC2 2025 Cybersecurity Workforce Study stopped publishing a headcount "gap" figure entirely. Organisations now report skills shortages (59% cite critical or significant skills needs) rather than a shortage of bodies. The old "millions of unfilled jobs, just show up" narrative is dead
  • In the same study, 39% of respondents reported hiring freezes, 36% budget cuts and 24% layoffs in their cybersecurity teams during 2025
  • Many postings labelled entry-level ask for a few years of experience. That is the "mid-level paradox": the demand is real, but it sits just above where you start
  • Agentic AI is absorbing repetitive Tier-1 triage work, so juniors are expected to arrive with more judgement and more automation ability than five years ago

The counter-move: stop optimising for certificate count and start optimising for evidence. A documented home lab, a working SIEM project, five thoughtful writeups, one internship, and three people in the industry who know your name will beat a stack of certifications with nothing behind them. Every hiring signal in 2026 points the same direction: show, don't claim.

Vocabulary You'll Be Expected to Know

You don't need deep expertise in these yet. You do need to not look blank when an interviewer says them.

CNAPP Cloud-Native App Protection Platform

The consolidation of CSPM, CWPP, CIEM and KSPM into a single platform. Vendors are collapsing categories fast.

Why it matters: it is how cloud security is actually bought and staffed now

CTEM Continuous Threat Exposure Management

Gartner's framing for continuously discovering, prioritising and validating exposure rather than running periodic scans.

Why it matters: reframes vulnerability management as an ongoing programme

ASPM App Security Posture Management

Correlating findings across SAST, DAST, SCA and runtime into one prioritised view of application risk.

Why it matters: it is how a large AppSec programme decides what to fix first

ITDR Identity Threat Detection & Response

Detection and response focused specifically on identity abuse: token theft, MFA fatigue, privilege escalation.

Why it matters: 82% of detections were malware-free in CrowdStrike's 2026 report, so the intruder often looks like a user

NHI Non-Human Identity

Service accounts, API keys, workload identities and now AI agent identities. In most estates they far outnumber human identities.

Why it matters: a growing gap in most IAM programmes

Detection-as-Code

Managing detection rules (Sigma, YARA, SIEM queries) like software: version controlled, peer reviewed, CI/CD tested.

Why it matters: it is the core practice of the detection engineering role

PQC Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST finalised ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA in 2024. Guidance deprecates RSA/ECC after 2030 and disallows them by 2035.

Why it matters: migration and crypto-agility roles are already being advertised

Zero Trust

Never trust, always verify: per-request authorisation instead of network-location trust. CISA's maturity model v2.0 is the common reference.

Why it matters: most security architecture conversations now start from it

Prompt Injection

Manipulating an LLM's behaviour through crafted input, still #1 on the OWASP LLM Top 10, and unsolved in the general case.

Why it matters: the signature vulnerability class of the AI era

Excessive Agency

Giving an AI agent more permissions, tools or autonomy than its task requires: the AI-era version of least privilege failure.

Why it matters: rising fast as organisations deploy agentic systems

SBOM / AIBOM

A machine-readable inventory of software (or model) components and their provenance. CERT-In issued Indian guidance in 2025.

Why it matters: supply chain is now A03 in the OWASP Top 10:2025

Purple Team

Red and blue working together: attacks run deliberately to build and validate detections, rather than to score points.

Why it matters: it is how detections get tested against real attack behaviour
